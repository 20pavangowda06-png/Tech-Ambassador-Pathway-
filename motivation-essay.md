# Part C — Technical Community Motivation Essay

*(~250 words — drafted for GitHub Campus Experts / HackerRank Campus Crew
applications)*

---

My first hackathon taught me something no classroom did: the students
around me were hungry to build, but most didn't know where to start. I
watched talented classmates stall at environment setup while others raced
ahead — not for lack of ability, but for lack of someone who had been
through it to point the way. That gap is why I want to be a student tech
ambassador.

This semester I built my foundations in public: nineteen HackerRank
problems accepted for a 3-star Problem Solving badge, nine documented
LeetCode solutions on GitHub, and a line editor in C built with a teammate
— every commit small, tested, and reviewed. I know what it feels like to
go from stuck to shipping, and I want to shorten that journey for others
on my campus.

As a Campus Expert and Crew member, I would run what I wish had existed:
weekly problem-solving circles where beginners tackle one DSA pattern at a
time, Git and GitHub workshops so nobody loses work to a bad merge again,
and a campus coding contest that gives first-years their first taste of
competitive programming. REVA has the talent; it needs the scaffolding.

I am applying not because I have all the answers, but because I was
recently the beginner — and I remember exactly what helped. If I can turn
one confused fresher into a confident contributor, that is the whole job.
